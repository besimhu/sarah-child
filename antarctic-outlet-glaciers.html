MANUSCRIPT PREVIEW · 2026 EDITOR REVISION · EAST ANTARCTICA

Nearly 60 years
of steady heights?

What can historical trimetrogon airphotos reveal about seven outlet glaciers flowing through the Transantarctic Mountains?

This work is labeled as a manuscript in editorial revision in the supplied file, not as a published article.

The manuscript title poses a careful question: what do historical photographs say about elevation change at these outlet glaciers across nearly six decades?

The supplied 2026 editor-revision manuscript is by Sarah F. Child and collaborators. It focuses on seven major outlet glaciers in the Transantarctic Mountains and uses trimetrogon aerial photography from 1960–64 to assemble an elevation record, which the authors compare with modern digital elevation models.

What the manuscript reports

In its abstract, the manuscript reports small elevation differences between 1960 and 2017, of approximately 6 m in magnitude. It identifies the largest changes at Nimrod Glacier (gain) and Amundsen Glacier (loss), and the smallest at Byrd Glacier.

This is a result from the supplied editor-revision manuscript, not a final published finding. Its interpretation depends on the methods and uncertainties discussed in the full paper, and may be refined during editorial review.

The manuscript describes relatively small, but not identical, elevation changes among seven Transantarctic outlet glaciers.

Why return to old imagery?

Observations of Antarctic glaciers before the satellite era are sparse. Trimetrogon aerial photography captures broad terrain coverage from an aircraft. With structure-from-motion photogrammetry, overlapping images can be reconstructed into historical elevation surfaces and compared with modern satellite-derived topography.

The supplied manuscript is marked as an editor revision. The story here previews the paper’s question, approach, and abstract-level result; it does not present this as a completed, published article.
